    <p class="paper-kicker">Job Market Paper</p>
    <h3>Public Activism and Labor-related Corporate Disclosure</h3>
    <p class="paper-authors">Jiamin Zhao <span aria-hidden="true">&middot;</span> Single-authored</p>
    <p class="paper-status">Working paper <span aria-hidden="true">&middot;</span> Draft available on request</p>
    <p class="paper-question">How does public labor activism near corporate headquarters shape labor-related disclosure in earnings calls?</p>
    <div class="paper-links">
      <a href="#job-market-abstract">Abstract <span aria-hidden="true">&rarr;</span></a>
      <a href="mailto:JZhao@iese.edu?subject=Job%20market%20paper%20draft%20request">Request draft <span aria-hidden="true">&rarr;</span></a>
    </div>
    <div class="paper-abstract" id="job-market-abstract">
      <h4>Abstract</h4>
      <p>This paper examines how the surrounding public environment shapes labor-related corporate disclosure. Greater non-firm-specific labor public activism around corporate headquarters is associated with less discussion of current labor-related operating conditions relative to talent-management information in subsequent earnings calls. The findings show that firms adjust disclosure to changes in the surrounding public environment even when the underlying activism does not directly target the firm.</p>
    </div>

  <article class="research-paper" id="ngo-activism">
    <p class="paper-kicker">Working paper</p>
    <h3>NGO Activism and the Supply of Corporate Information</h3>
    <p class="paper-authors">Jiamin Zhao <span aria-hidden="true">&middot;</span> Single-authored</p>
    <p class="paper-status">Working paper <span aria-hidden="true">&middot;</span> Draft available on request</p>
    <p class="paper-question">How does NGO targeting change what managers discuss with capital markets?</p>
    <div class="paper-links">
      <a href="#ngo-abstract">Abstract <span aria-hidden="true">&rarr;</span></a>
      <a href="mailto:JZhao@iese.edu?subject=NGO%20activism%20paper%20draft%20request">Request draft <span aria-hidden="true">&rarr;</span></a>
    </div>
    <div class="paper-abstract" id="ngo-abstract">
      <h4>Abstract</h4>
      <p>This paper examines how NGO activism shapes managerial disclosure to capital markets. Using U.S. NGO targeting events and earnings-call transcripts, I find that environmental targeting is associated with greater climate-transition disclosure, while labor targeting is associated with less discussion of labor operating conditions. The findings suggest that NGO activism can selectively reshape investor-facing information supply, highlighting the role of shareholder-stakeholder interest alignment in external corporate governance.</p>
    </div>

  <article class="research-paper" id="automated-access">
    <p class="paper-kicker">Working paper</p>
    <h3>How Firms Govern Automated Data Access: Evidence from Robots.txt Policies</h3>
    <p class="paper-authors">Jiamin Zhao, Long Cheng, and Igor Kadach</p>
    <p class="paper-status">Working paper <span aria-hidden="true">&middot;</span> Draft available on request</p>
    <p class="paper-question">How do firms govern automated access to information that remains publicly available?</p>
    <div class="paper-links">
      <a href="#automated-access-abstract">Abstract <span aria-hidden="true">&rarr;</span></a>
      <a href="mailto:JZhao@iese.edu?subject=Automated%20data%20access%20paper%20draft%20request">Request draft <span aria-hidden="true">&rarr;</span></a>
    </div>
    <div class="paper-abstract" id="automated-access-abstract">
      <h4>Abstract</h4>
      <p>This paper constructs a novel historical measure of corporate digital information-access policies using archived web-access directives for U.S.-listed firms over the 2001-2024 period. We document substantial and persistent heterogeneity in how firms govern automated access to publicly available information, including whether restrictions apply broadly or selectively to named agents. We validate the measure using externally documented changes in web-access rules and characterize the long-run evolution of firms' machine-access policies. The evidence identifies automated access as a distinct dimension of corporate information policy: public availability does not imply unrestricted machine access, and firms actively govern both margins.</p>
    </div>
